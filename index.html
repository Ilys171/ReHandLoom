Engineering Prototype

Protecting the hands that keep the loom alive.

ReHand Loom is a wearable hand-and-wrist rehabilitation system for rural Azerbaijani carpet weavers. Years of repetitive knotting, cutting and sustained wrist positioning can turn minor numbness and tired hands into lasting loss of grip and fine motor control — often far from specialist care. ReHand Loom brings monitoring and guided home rehabilitation to the weaver's own house.

Background & problem

A heritage craft, carried on strained hands.

Azerbaijani carpet weaving is inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, and remains a family-based livelihood for women across rural regions. The craft demands years of repetitive knotting, cutting and sustained wrist positioning.

Early symptoms — numbness, tingling, fatigue — are easy to dismiss as "just tired hands." Left unaddressed, they can progress toward carpal-tunnel-type dysfunction and a lasting loss of grip strength and fine motor control. Outside major cities, specialist follow-up means costly, time-consuming travel, so early symptoms are often normalized until function is already impaired.

  • Specialist care concentrated in cities, far from weaving villages
  • Early strain signs routinely normalized, not assessed
  • Progressive risk to grip strength and fine motor control

Research question

Can a low-cost sensor glove turn everyday hand movement into a session profile a specialist can act on — remotely?

Mission

To make early hand rehabilitation more accessible, affordable and data-driven for underserved rural communities in Azerbaijan.

Vision

A scalable assistive-health technology system that helps prevent minor hand symptoms from becoming permanent functional problems — complementing, never replacing, in-person professional care.

Operating principle

A closed loop: sense, interpret, guide, track, review, adjust.

01

Calibrate

Each weaver's comfortable baseline is recorded — open hand, fist, neutral wrist.

02

Capture

Flexion, grip pressure and wrist motion are sampled at a fixed rate during weaving or exercise.

03

Process

Signals are filtered, normalised against the calibration and reduced to clinical features.

04

Review

A specialist examines trends and flagged patterns remotely — never raw signals.

05

Guide

Advice and adjusted exercises return to the weaver through local follow-up.

Proposed system

A glove, a microcontroller, and an app that speaks Azerbaijani first.

Two flex sensors track finger bend and range, an MPU6050 IMU reads wrist angle and motion, and a thin-film pressure sensor proxies grip. An ESP32 buffers each session and streams it over Bluetooth LE to an offline-first mobile app with guided exercises, progress tracking and a daily symptom diary.

Sensing

2 flex sensors (10 kΩ dividers, scalable to 5), MPU6050 IMU, thin-film pressure sensor.

Processing

ESP32, Bluetooth LE, on-device offline buffering for unreliable connectivity.

Power

USB power bank in the current prototype; on-board battery planned.

Software

Flutter application, offline-first, Azerbaijani-first with guided routines.

Validation strategy

Built with weavers, checked against their movements.

  • Bench calibration

    Sensor range, drift and repeatability measured before any human trial.

  • Controlled movements

    Known motions compared against recorded signals.

  • Wearability & usability

    Comfort, fit and calibration time evaluated with real weavers.

  • Specialist co-design

    Outputs, thresholds and risk flags refined with rehabilitation professionals.

Pilot study protocol

15–25 weavers · 6–8 weeks · 2–3 villages

Weekly records combine exercise adherence, symptom self-reports and sensor summaries. Only anonymised, aggregate data are exported — progress is always measured against each weaver's own baseline, from a recorded baseline through a week 4 midpoint review to a week 8 post-assessment.

Requirements were informed by occupational-therapy consultation and WHO assistive-technology guidance. Development runs in four phases — research & requirements, hardware prototype, app & data layer, and pilot & iteration — with the wearable assembly and bench calibration currently in progress.

Data protection & ethics

A support tool, never a diagnosis.

Participation is voluntary and based on informed consent. Sensor data are pseudonymised, and only aggregate, anonymised summaries are exported for research. Automated analysis supports interpretation, while trained specialists retain final responsibility for every recommendation.

ReHand Loom extends access to monitoring and specialist review — it does not replace professional assessment.
